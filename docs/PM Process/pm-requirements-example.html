Aha feature · SYM-2566

92970 — Change wall thickness per wall — Implement

Background

Claims Estimate (formerly Mobile Claims) and Estimate Mobile (iOS) include a diagram tool that allows users to sketch floor plans of a loss site. Wall thickness is controlled via Floorplan Properties, which provides two global default settings: one for interior wall thickness (default 4.0 inches) and one for exterior wall thickness (default 4.0 inches). The tool itself warns users that changing the interior wall thickness will affect the dimensions of all existing rooms on the floor plan.

Both settings are global — the interior thickness value applies uniformly to every interior wall in the floor plan, and the exterior thickness value applies uniformly to every exterior wall. There is no mechanism to override the thickness of an individual wall independently of these global defaults.

Market Problem

Residential properties with additions — rooms or wings built onto the original structure — commonly have walls of different thicknesses. The original home's framing may use different construction than the addition. When a user diagrams such a property today, they face an unresolvable conflict: the global wall thickness settings cannot be correct for both sections of the home simultaneously.

This breaks estimate accuracy at its foundation. Wall thickness directly feeds into room dimension calculations, which determine square footage, and therefore drive line items, material quantities, and final costs. A miscalculated wall thickness compounds across every affected room — creating leakage risk, estimate disputes with contractors and PAs, and re-work when the error is caught downstream.

The problem has been documented since May 2015 (Case 1210465) and is requested across 13 carrier clients including Liberty Mutual, Erie, QBE, and Donegal — a signal that this is not an edge case but a common field scenario. The inability to resolve it within the tool forces users to apply manual workarounds outside the software, introducing the exact data integrity and accuracy risks the platform is designed to eliminate.

Business connection: Wall thickness feeds directly into square footage, which drives every material quantity and line-item cost in the estimate. A systematic error at this layer is a direct source of indemnity leakage — overpayments when dimensions are inflated, estimate disputes when contractors or PAs identify the gap, and re-work costs when errors are corrected late in the cycle. Resolving this closes a documented competitive gap and directly supports retention for high-value carrier accounts.

Phase 3 — Objectives

Claude derives the objectives from the blockers identified in Phase 2. Each bullet maps to one or more specific blocker rows — making scope explicit and giving the PM a natural de-scoping checkpoint before requirements are written.

Phase 3 · Objectives
  • Enable per-wall thickness overrides in the diagram tool. Users must be able to set a wall thickness on an individual wall that differs from the global Floorplan Properties default, so that properties with additions or mixed-framing construction can be diagrammed accurately without compromising any other wall's dimensions. (Resolves blockers at Steps 2, 4, 5.)
  • Preserve global wall thickness settings as the default. The existing Floorplan Properties interior and exterior wall thickness settings must continue to function as the floor-plan-wide defaults. A per-wall override applies only to the targeted wall and does not trigger recalculation of any other wall, eliminating the current all-or-nothing conflict. (Resolves Step 5 blocker.)
  • Eliminate the phantom room created by the gap workaround. By making accurate per-wall thickness representable directly in the diagram, users have no need to leave deliberate gaps between rooms. This removes a root cause of inflated square footage, incorrect line items, and downstream re-work. (Resolves Step 6 blocker.)
  • Maintain estimate accuracy and traceability for complex properties. Dimension calculations for every room must reflect the actual wall thickness applied to each bounding wall, so that square footage, material quantities, and resulting line items are correct by construction — not manually corrected after the fact. (Resolves Step 7 blocker.)
  • Deliver parity with the competitive baseline. This capability has been requested by 13 carrier clients since 2015. Providing it closes a documented gap and directly supports client retention for accounts including Liberty Mutual, Erie, and QBE.

1 of 2 — Draft · Claims Estimate & Estimate Mobile (iOS)

Applies to: Claims Estimate, Estimate Mobile (iOS)

Statement: As a user, I want to set a custom wall thickness on an individual wall so that I can accurately represent properties where different sections have different wall construction without affecting the dimensions of any other wall.

Details:

  1. A wall thickness override option must be accessible by selecting an individual wall in the diagram — via context menu or wall properties panel on Claims Estimate; via tap-and-hold or wall properties panel consistent with existing iOS interaction patterns on Estimate Mobile.
  2. The per-wall thickness override must accept the same input format as the existing Floorplan Properties interior and exterior wall thickness fields: values entered in inches with up to one decimal place, adjustable in 0.5 inch increments via arrow controls or via manual entry. No minimum or maximum value is enforced beyond what the existing Floorplan Properties fields currently enforce.
  3. When a per-wall thickness override is applied, the system behavior depends on the wall type: (a) Exterior wall — one side of the wall bounds open space or the building exterior: interior dimensions of the bounded room are preserved exactly as entered. The system expands the wall outward, recalculating exterior dimensions only. (b) Interior shared wall — enclosed rooms on both sides: the system must prompt the user: Which room should absorb the thickness change? [Room A name] or [Room B name]? The selected room interior dimensions reduce by the thickness delta. The non-selected room interior dimensions are unchanged.
  4. If a wall borders two rooms and the user is prompted per Detail 3, only the room selected by the user is affected. The system must not silently modify any room the user did not select.
  5. When the global wall thickness is subsequently changed via Floorplan Properties, walls with a per-wall override retain their custom value and are not affected by the global change.
  6. A user must be able to remove a per-wall override and revert the wall to the current global default. On revert, the inverse logic of Detail 3 applies: exterior walls recalculate outward; interior shared walls prompt the user to select which room recovers the space.
  7. If a room containing an overridden wall is copied or duplicated, the copied wall inherits the custom thickness value.
  8. Per-wall thickness overrides set in Estimate Mobile must be preserved and correctly reflected when the diagram is synced to Claims Estimate.
  9. Wall thickness overrides must be applied to one wall at a time. Multi-wall selection for simultaneous thickness override is not supported. If a user attempts to select multiple walls before applying a thickness change, the override option must not be available until a single wall is selected.
  10. If applying a per-wall thickness override would cause one or more objects on the affected wall — doors, windows, openings, or blocks — to no longer fit within the available wall space, the system must display the existing validation warning before applying the change. The override must not be applied until the conflict is resolved. This detection must behave consistently with how the system currently handles the same conflict when changing interior or exterior wall thickness globally via Floorplan Properties.
  11. When a per-wall thickness override changes the dimensions of any bounded room, all quantity-dependent calculations for those rooms must be recalculated immediately, including but not limited to floor surface area and ceiling surface area. The updated quantities must be reflected in the estimate at the time the override is applied.

Example: A field tech diagrams a home where the Laundry and Garage share a wall. When they override that shared wall from 4 inches to 6 inches, the system prompts: Which room should absorb the thickness change? Laundry or Garage? The tech selects Laundry. The Laundry interior dimensions reduce by 2 inches, and its floor and ceiling surface quantities are recalculated immediately to reflect the reduced area. The Garage is untouched. On the other side of the home, overriding an exterior wall from 4 inches to 6 inches preserves the interior room dimensions, expands the building footprint outward, and recalculates affected surface quantities accordingly — no prompt required.

2 of 2 — Draft · Claims Estimate & Estimate Mobile (iOS)

Applies to: Claims Estimate, Estimate Mobile (iOS)

Statement: As a user, I want a wall with a custom thickness to render visually at its actual set thickness in the diagram so that the floor plan accurately reflects the real-world construction without requiring any additional visual markers.

Details:

  1. A wall with a per-wall thickness override must render in the diagram canvas at its actual set thickness — appearing visually thicker or thinner than adjacent walls that use a different thickness value, consistent with how the diagram can render exterior walls thicker than interior walls.
  2. The rendered thickness of the wall on the canvas must be proportional to its set value relative to other walls in the diagram.
  3. No additional visual treatment — such as a color change, line style change, or marker — is applied to distinguish an overridden wall from a default wall. The rendered thickness is the sole visual differentiator.
  4. The wall must render at its set thickness in both the diagram editing view and on printed and exported outputs.
  5. The rendered wall thickness must not obscure dimension labels or other wall annotations on the diagram canvas.

Example: A user overrides the wall between the Laundry and Garage from 4 inches to 6 inches. In the diagram, that wall renders visibly thicker than the surrounding 4-inch walls — the same way exterior walls can appear thicker than interior walls. The printed floor plan shows the same proportional thickness with no additional styling applied.
